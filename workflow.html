From data to drugs

The virtual screening pipeline

How a chemoinformatics workflow narrows an enormous compound library down to a handful of candidates worth testing in the lab.

Collect & curate

Pull structures and bioactivity from databases such as PubChem and ChEMBL. Standardise them: remove salts, fix charges, normalise tautomers and drop duplicates.

Encode

Convert each molecule into numbers — descriptors (MW, logP, TPSA) and fingerprints (path-based, ECFP/Morgan) — so algorithms can compare them.

Filter

Apply drug-likeness rules such as Lipinski's Rule of Five, and remove reactive or assay-interfering substructures (PAINS).

Search & model

Rank by similarity to known actives, dock into a protein target, or score with QSAR / machine-learning models trained on measured activities.

Select & test

Pick a diverse, high-scoring subset for synthesis and assays. Results feed back into the models — the design–make–test–analyse loop.
